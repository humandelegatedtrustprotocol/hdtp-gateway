# internal/node

The composition root of the public surface: the one place where a config, a store, a keyring and a limits client become a serving node (SPEC.md §2.1, §5). It wires and does not decide: authorization is `policy.Allow`, the seal and client-cert knobs are `public.Identifier`, source refusal is `public.LANGuard`, the tools are `public.BuiltinEntries`, the budgets are the limits sidecar's. What lives here is the assembly those parts assume (per-account keypairs and certificates, one registry, pool and identifier per account, SNI certificate selection, routes, the listener lifecycle) and the node's outbound half (sending messages, calling contacts, refreshing a card, announcing a move). Callers: `internal/cli` (`serve`, `compose.go`, `contactinit.go`, the admin commands), the portal and owner-MCP services (`internal/services/...`, `internal/internalui`), and `internal/integrations`, which hand it integration tools through `SetIntegrationTools`. It calls `internal/public`, `internal/outbound`, `internal/identity` (the key and leaf manager), `internal/contacts`, `internal/messaging`, `internal/limits`, `internal/tunnel` (source address, probe), `internal/ingress` (`PinOnwardLeg`), `internal/core` and its store.

## What it holds

Assembly and lifecycle (node.go):
- `Options`, `New`, `Node`: `New` builds every account (a key that will not open fails startup unless another account proves the master key; see below), the `public.Server`, and wraps the handler as body cap, then LAN guard, then routes.
- `Start`, `Stop`, `Addr`, `Handler`, `TLSConfig`, `MaxBodyBytes`, `PublicHeaderTimeout`, `PublicRequestTimeout`, `PublicAnswerTimeout`, `PublicIdleTimeout`, `PublicMaxHeaderBytes`: the listener (connection cap beneath TLS), its bounds, and its TLS posture (SPEC.md §5.1: request a client certificate, never require; with an ingress fingerprint configured, `ingress.PinOnwardLeg` requires the paired ingress's certificate).
- `AdoptAccount`, `ForgetAccount`, `RetireExpiredLeaves`, `WithoutCampaign`, `Follow`, `Slugs`, `AwaitingLeaf`, `Unavailable`, `ErrAwaitingLeaf`, `ErrCampaignWalking`: the live set of accounts; changes made by another process on the store reach this one through `Follow`.
- Accessors the portal and owner MCP use: `Card`, `SignCard`, `CertificateInfo`, `Certificate`, `Messages`, `Contacts`, `Pool`, `Bus`, `ServedPermissions`, `PublicURL`/`SetPublicURL`/`UsePublicURL`, `LANAllowed`/`SetLANConnections`/`UseLANConnections`, `SetSeal`, `Invalidate`, `InvalidateAccount`, `SetIntegrationTools`, `ContactCap`, `LimitsAnswer`, `LandingDeps`.

Budgets (node.go, outbound.go): the node turns what a call is into the charges the limits sidecar decides (`chargeOf`, `consumeBudget`, `admit`, `decide`), and `IntegrationBudget` wraps an integration tool with its per-contact cap. `spendOutbound` charges calls out.

Outbound (outbound.go, send.go, contactcall.go, request.go, refresh.go, announce.go, campaign.go):
- `PeerOf`, `OutboundClient`: the `outbound.Peer` for a pinned contact and a client wired to the store, the clock, the budget and the pin write-backs.
- `SendMessage`, `SendMedia`, `RetryPending`, `RunRetries`, and the `Status*`, `RetrySweep`, `MaxRetryBatch`, `MaxRetryDelay` constants: record first, then deliver; the retry sweep resends with the same `msg_id`.
- `ListContactTools`, `CallContact`, `ContactTool`, `RequestContact`, `ErrRequestRefused`, `RefreshContact`, `ContactRefresh`, the `Refresh*` outcomes, `FetchMedia`.
- `AnnounceMove`, `ResumeMove`, `MoveProgress`, `MoveUnreached`: the durable fan-out that tells contacts of a new leaf.

## What it refuses, and how

- `New`: errors for a missing `Store`, `Landing` or `Limits`; and when no account could be built while at least one account's key failed to open and none proved the master key, it refuses with a message that names both readings (wrong master key, or one lost for good) and the recovery commands. An account awaiting its leaf is skipped and audited (`account_awaiting_leaf`); one that cannot be built is recorded in `Unavailable` and audited (`account_unavailable`); the others serve.
- Budgets: every sealed call is charged to the account it is addressed to. An active contact whose proof the envelope did not demote spends its own bucket and the account's aggregate; any other proven root spends the guest budget of that root at its source; nothing proven spends the source alone; an opened call that proved no active or pending_out contact also spends the account's guest total. A sidecar that does not answer is `unavailable` (audited `limits_unavailable`); a refused bucket is `rate_limited` (audited with the bucket). The guest total is checked before the open (`admit`). The pending-request cap comes from the same sidecar and makes `request_contact` answer `unavailable`.
- Calls out (`spendOutbound`): a call to an active contact spends that contact's outbound bucket and the aggregate; to anybody else, or with `request_contact`, `redeem_invite`, `contact_accepted`, `contact_rejected`, the account's stranger budget. A refusal sends nothing and returns `*outbound.RateLimited`, or an `unavailable:` error when the sidecar is down.
- Sending: only to an `active` contact (`send: contact is <status>, not active`, `send: unknown contact`). The endpoint is checked before every delivery (`checkEndpoint`): it must parse, must be `https`, and a private or loopback IP literal is refused when no key is pinned for the contact (a pinned contact is dialled wherever its leaf says, since the handshake decides who answers). A refusal is audited `delivery` / `endpoint_refused`. A peer's refusal of `send_message` or `send_media` is an error (`send: the contact's node refused: <code>`) and the message stays pending.
- Delivery timing: one attempt on the caller's behalf is bounded to 30 seconds (and survives the caller's cancellation); the message is already recorded. The retry sweep runs every 15 seconds, takes at most 128 messages a pass, doubles the delay per attempt up to 30 minutes, and gives up at the message's deadline (`expires`, or 24 hours after creation, `store.DefaultMessageExpiry`) by marking it `failed` and auditing `expired`. Only the process that holds the retry lease sweeps (`RunRetries`' `leading`).
- `RefreshContact`: peer-caused failures are outcomes, never errors. `unreachable` (no usable answer), `refused` (the answer did not verify: no signed card, a chain member not base64url, not exactly two certificates, the chain failing HDTP §14.2 at the pinned root and the pinned endpoint, a leaf older than the pinned one, two leaves with the same `notBefore`, a card that does not name the pinned root or carry the proven leaf, a bad card signature), `renewed` (a newer leaf under the pinned root, re-pinned at the same address), `updated` (a different card under the same leaf), `unchanged`. An error is returned only when the refresh could not be attempted or recorded. A refresh never moves an address: the chain is validated at the pinned endpoint, and a chain valid at another address is SPEC.md §5.3's business, not a pull's.
- Move campaign: `update_contact` carrying the new card, chain form; `pending_approval` counts as reached; a refusing peer is asked with `request_contact` only if this campaign owes the contact the handshake, with the contact marked `pending_out` first and taken back if the request did not land; a peer that answers a refusal other than `rate_limited`/`unavailable`/none is recorded `refused` and not asked again for that leaf. One walk per identity at a time (`campaigns`, `ErrCampaignWalking`).
- SNI: a certificate is chosen by the host a leaf's endpoint names, else by account slug, else by the slug in the first label, else the first account's by slug order. Two accounts naming one host: the first keeps it and the clash is audited `account_host_clash`.
- The tunnelled public handler turns off the MCP SDK's DNS-rebinding protection (the surface is always TLS), and the SDK's body limit is set to `MaxBodyBytes` (8 MiB, SPEC.md §5.7).

## Invariants

- Record, then deliver: a message the owner typed survives a failed send; a delivered status is written even if the caller has gone, so the retry loop does not send it twice.
- The same `msg_id` is reused across every retry, which is what makes the retry safe (SPEC.md §7.1, §7.2).
- The seal value the gate enforces and the card advertises is one cell per account; `SetSeal` writes the effective value, never the requested one, and removes or reinstalls `sealed_call` in the registry.
- A request's pin checks for a client chain run once per request (`resolveTransport`) and the pool's gate enforces that result.
- An account is served only under a leaf that has not run out: an expired leaf's key is destroyed and the account is taken out of the indexes and listed as awaiting a leaf.
- Outbound identity has one home: everything that leaves on the owner's behalf goes through `OutboundClient`, whose `Roots` stays nil (the system roots; an empty pool would trust nothing).
- `Follow` applies another process's invalidations and account changes and skips what this process published itself.

## What it does not do

- It does not decide authorization, budgets or what an envelope proves; those are `policy`, the limits sidecar and `public.Identifier`.
- It does not relay or queue for a third party: delivery is direct and only direct (HDTP §9); a peer that cannot be reached before the message's deadline is a reported failure.
- It does not go looking for changes to a contact's card or address: a refresh is one contact, on request; a new leaf arrives on use (HDTP §14.3).
- Saving a new public URL is not a move and announces nothing: `SetPublicURL` changes the base the next certificate request will name and moves nobody (a leaf still names the address it was issued for).
- It does not fetch a peer's media URL on its own; `FetchMedia` is the owner's explicit action (SPEC.md §7.5).
- It does not apply the TLS or the connection cap in `Handler`; `Start` adds them.
